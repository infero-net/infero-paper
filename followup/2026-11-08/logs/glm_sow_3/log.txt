Second pass through the handbook — adding what the first pass didn't cover rather than repeating it:

- **Housing density caveat:** 2.0 m²/sow is on the tight side. The EU minimum for group-housed sows (Directive 2008/120/EC) is 2.25 m², with ±10% adjustments for very small or very large groups. Worth checking against local rules; extra space also pays off in reduced aggression and injuries, especially in dynamic groups.
- **"Adjust to body condition" needs a concrete anchor:** the standard tool is a 1–5 body condition score, with a target around 3.0–3.25 at farrowing. Over-conditioned sows eat less in lactation and lose more reserves; under-conditioned sows carry smaller subsequent litters. The 2.0–2.5 kg gestation range is a starting point — the real controller is the BCS trend across parities.
- **Water delivery rate is a silent failure mode:** 30 L/day in lactation is a reasonable target, but nipple drinkers flowing under ~2 L/min effectively ration high-yielding sows regardless of the stated target. A drinker flow check belongs in the daily routine.
- **Creep temperature should be a schedule, not a fixed value:** 30 °C is right for day 1, stepping down roughly 2 °C per week. A permanently hot creep discourages older piglets from using it, which pushes them toward the sow and raises crushing risk — undercutting the stated mortality goal.
- **Wash the sows, not just the pens:** hygiene covers pens between batches, but sows entering the farrowing room are the main pathogen vector. Washing sows at the 5-day pre-farrowing move-in closes the loop. (Also: the move-in date presupposes accurate service records — the 114-day "3 months, 3 weeks, 3 days" rule is the scheduling backbone behind it.)
- **Straw does double duty:** manipulable material for welfare/enrichment *and* the fiber/constipation goal — a genuine two-for-one, though it conflicts with fully slatted floors and drain hygiene in some systems.
- **Records should map to the stated goal:** to actually measure "healthy sows, large litters, low piglet mortality," the record step needs at minimum: total born / born alive / stillborn, piglet deaths by cause and day, weaning weight, and weaning-to-estrus interval (target 4–7 days; longer flags a problem). Pre-weaning mortality under ~10–12% is a realistic benchmark.

Third pass — covering the remaining gaps, then I think this material is fully mined:

- **Colostrum is the missing keystone:** The farrowing section handles thermoregulation (20 °C room, 30 °C creep) but says nothing about the first 12–16 hours after birth, which is where pre-weaning mortality is actually won or lost. Drying piglets at birth, split suckling for large litters, and confirming colostrum intake are the interventions that make the creep-lamp investment pay off. A warm, dry, unfed piglet still dies.
- **Cross-fostering absent:** Equalizing litter sizes within ~24 h (moving piglets from large litters to sows with smaller, same-age litters) is a standard, cheap lever directly serving the "low piglet mortality" goal. Worth adding alongside the farrowing protocol, with the caveat that piglets should get colostrum from their biological dam first.
- **Group housing + restricted feeding = intake competition:** A subtle tension inside the handbook itself — restricted gestation diets (2.0–2.5 kg) in group pens let dominant sows over-eat and subordinates under-eat, driving BCS divergence that undercuts "adjust to body condition." Mitigations: individual feeding stations, or at minimum spreading floor feeding widely and evenly. This compounds with the 2.0 m² tightness noted last pass.
- **The "..." in the daily routine** presumably hides enrichment provision, temperature checks, and scheduled health work (vaccination, deworming). Those should be made explicit, because steps that aren't written down are the ones that don't happen — and the record step can't capture what wasn't done.
- **Observation needs a post-farrowing focus:** "Observe sows" in the first 3 days post-farrowing should specifically mean checking for MMA/agalactia — hard udder, off-feed, restless piglets. Early detection is the difference between a treated sow and a lost litter; late detection is catastrophic.
- **Crate context:** The crate-with-creep is the conventional anti-crushing design and consistent with the mortality goal, but loose or hybrid farrowing systems are advancing through legislation and buyer expectations in several regions. Same posture as the m² point: verify against current local rules before relying on the handbook as final.
